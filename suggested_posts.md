Ajay Goel, Co-Founder and CEO International Business at SUN Mobility, said the open system would give riders and fleet operators greater flexibility while providing financiers with confidence that electric vehicles would have access to reliable charging infrastructure. “Kenya is just the beginning of our long-term vision to build Africa's largest universal battery swapping network for electric mobility,” Goel said. SUN Mobility said its platform includes Smart Batteries, Quick Interchange Stations and a cloud-based Smart Network that manages battery assets, customer connections, tracking and maintenance. The company has showcased compatible vehicles from more than 10 manufacturers, including Afrina Neopower, BGauss, Fika Mobility, Motovolt, Odysse, Piaggio, QJ-YY, Sprocomm, VMoto and Wylex. A new report has revealed that mobile money dominates Kenya's fraud landscape, emerging as the leading channel exploited by criminals to con unsuspecting Kenyans out of their money. The findings are contained in a report by the National Computer and Cybercrimes Coordination Committee (NC4) obtained by Kenyans.co.ke on August 24, detailing the most common fraud schemes and channels used in the country. According to the report, mobile money fraud accounted for 18.6 per cent of all cases recorded, making it one of the most prevalent forms of fraud targeting Kenyans. Mobile money platforms also emerged as the preferred destination for illicit funds, featuring in 51 of the 102 reported cases reviewed in the report. That works out to exactly half of all documented fraud cases in the first six months of 2026, showing just how central phone-based transactions have become to criminal operations. On the other hand, investment and forex schemes followed closely behind mobile money fraud, accounting for 16 cases, translating to 15.7 per cent of the total cases documented by the committee. Cryptocurrency-related scams came in third, with 12 recorded cases representing 11.8 per cent, a figure that mirrors crypto's growing footprint as both a fraud scheme and a payment channel. Account takeover and impersonation cases followed with 10 incidents, making up 9.8 per cent, while online shopping fraud trailed slightly with nine cases at 8.8 per cent of the total. Fake websites and phishing attempts accounted for eight cases, representing 7.8 per cent, as criminals increasingly rely on cloned platforms to trick Kenyans into surrendering personal details. Impersonation targeting brands, individuals, and government agencies made up seven cases at 6.9 per cent, while advance fee schemes, where victims pay before receiving promised benefits, recorded six cases at 5.9 per cent. Recruitment and job scams accounted for four cases at 3.9 per cent, followed by SIM swap fraud with three cases, representing 2.9 per cent of all documented incidents. On the payment side, bank transfers were the second most common channel after mobile money, linked to 22 cases and accounting for 21.6 per cent of all transactions traced.
