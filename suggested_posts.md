**1. Sakaja Explains Alleged Ksh8,000 Fee for Content Creators Filming in Nairobi**

Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja has addressed concerns surrounding new charges imposed on filmmakers, content creators and other players in the city's creative industry under the Nairobi City County Finance Act, 2026. The charges have sparked debate among content creators and digital entertainment players, with concerns emerging over the potential cost of producing and commercialising content in Nairobi. The new fees, which were highlighted in the 2026 Finance Act on Monday, cover a range of filming and digital entertainment activities, including commercial productions, content creation studios, streaming platforms and monetised events. Sakaja has now sought to clarify the county's position, saying the charges were not designed with ordinary digital creators in mind. “These charges were never intended to target ordinary content creators, influencers or young people creating digital content. We intend to regulate professional and commercial film productions that require significant use of public spaces and infrastructure,” Sakaja said. Under the 2026 Finance Act, local producers will pay Ksh8,000 per commercial filming session, while external commercial filming will attract Ksh50,000 per shooting. Religious and private filming will cost Ksh8,000, while music-video production will attract Ksh10,000. Further, the Act also provides for an annual Ksh40,000 charge for content creation studios, while local streaming platforms will pay Ksh100,000 and digital content platforms Ksh80,000 annually. Influencers holding monetised events will also pay Ksh10,000 per event. Sakaja said the county distinguishes between an individual creating ordinary social media content and a large commercial production involving crews, specialised equipment, multiple vehicles and extensive occupation of public spaces. “There is a clear difference between a content creator shooting a video for social media and a large film production arriving with a crew, equipment, vehicles and requiring the temporary closure or controlled use of a road. That is who we are targeting,” he said. He further stated that his administration recognises the creative sector as an important source of livelihoods, particularly for young people, as well as a platform for showcasing and marketing Nairobi to local and international audiences.

**2. New Report Reveals Leading Channels Used by Fraudsters in Kenya**

A new report has revealed that mobile money dominates Kenya's fraud landscape, emerging as the leading channel exploited by criminals to con unsuspecting Kenyans out of their money. The findings are contained in a report by the National Computer and Cybercrimes Coordination Committee (NC4) obtained by Kenyans.co.ke on August 24, detailing the most common fraud schemes and channels used in the country. According to the report, mobile money fraud accounted for 18.6 per cent of all cases recorded, making it one of the most prevalent forms of fraud targeting Kenyans. Mobile money platforms also emerged as the preferred destination for illicit funds, featuring in 51 of the 102 reported cases reviewed in the report. That works out to exactly half of all documented fraud cases in the first six months of 2026, showing just how central phone-based transactions have become to criminal operations. On the other hand, investment and forex schemes followed closely behind mobile money fraud, accounting for 16 cases, translating to 15.7 per cent of the total cases documented by the committee. Cryptocurrency-related scams came in third, with 12 recorded cases representing 11.8 per cent, a figure that mirrors crypto's growing footprint as both a fraud scheme and a payment channel. Account takeover and impersonation cases followed with 10 incidents, making up 9.8 per cent, while online shopping fraud trailed slightly with nine cases at 8.8 per cent of the total. Fake websites and phishing attempts accounted for eight cases, representing 7.8 per cent, as criminals increasingly rely on cloned platforms to trick Kenyans into surrendering personal details. Impersonation targeting brands, individuals, and government agencies made up seven cases at 6.9 per cent, while advance fee schemes, where victims pay before receiving promised benefits, recorded six cases at 5.9 per cent. Recruitment and job scams accounted for four cases at 3.9 per cent, followed by SIM swap fraud with three cases, representing 2.9 per cent of all documented incidents. On the payment side, bank transfers were the second most common channel after mobile money, linked to 22 cases and accounting for 21.6 per cent of all transactions traced.

**7. IEBC Explains Education Requirements to Contest Elective Seats in 2027**

The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) has clarified that a university degree is currently not required for Kenyans seeking to contest for president, governor or Member of Parliament in the 2027 General Election. IEBC Commissioner Ann Nderitu said the position follows court decisions that declared the degree requirement contained in the Elections Act unconstitutional, meaning the commission cannot currently enforce it during nominations. “As we start now, there is no requirement for a degree for anybody. That's the situation we are in,” Nderitu said during an interview on Citizen TV. The clarification means aspirants without university degrees can seek elective positions in the 2027 polls, provided they meet the other constitutional and statutory requirements governing eligibility for their respective offices. Candidates must still satisfy requirements including Kenyan citizenship, registration as voters, nomination by a political party or qualification to run as independent candidates, and compliance with leadership and integrity provisions. They must also meet other requirements specific to the position they are seeking. Nderitu added that the degree requirement had previously been challenged in court, with petitioners questioning whether requiring university qualifications for presidential, parliamentary and gubernatorial candidates was constitutional. She said the courts considered Article 27 of the Constitution, which guarantees equality and freedom from discrimination, as well as Article 10, which requires public participation in the making of laws and policies. Further, the court also considered the number of Kenyans holding university degrees, citing the 2019 Census figure of 3.5 per cent. She said the ruling found that the requirement could effectively exclude the remaining 96.5 per cent of Kenyans from contesting for key elective positions. “According to the ruling, it means that the rest of the Kenyan 96.5 per cent cannot participate in elections for governor, for president and for member of parliament. Meaning then, that is discriminatory,” she said. She further explained that the degree requirement could be introduced again, but only through a proper legislative process that allows Kenyans to participate and give their views .

**8. Govt plans to rename KUSCCO to KEFESCO after Sh13bn loss**

NAIROBI, Kenya, Aug 24 – The Government plans to rename the Kenya Union of Savings and Credit Cooperatives (KUSCCO) to the Kenya Federation of Savings and Credit Cooperatives (KEFESCO) following a financial crisis that left Saccos with billions of shillings in losses. Cooperative Development Commissioner David Obonyo announced the proposed name change in a notice to KUSCCO shareholders ahead of a special general meeting scheduled for August 28. The meeting will also receive a report from the board on the status of the union and consider other matters concerning its operations. “Pursuant to the provisions of Sec. 27 (8) of the Cooperative Societies Act; read together with rule 20(2) and rule 21(2) of the Cooperative Societies rules, the commissioner for cooperative development may convene a special general meeting of a society at which he may direct the matters to be discussed in the meeting,” Obonyo said. “This is therefore to notify all shareholders of Kenya Union of Savings and Credit Cooperatives (KUSCCO) Ltd that a special general meeting of the Union will be held on 28th August,2026 commencing at 9.00 am at All Saints Cathedral Church Hall to discuss the following agenda. Each shareholder of the SACCO is requested to send (2) two representatives for this meeting.” The proposed restructuring follows a PwC audit in 2025 that uncovered an estimated financial hole of between Sh12 billion and Sh13.3 billion at KUSCCO. The losses were linked to ghost loans, falsified commissions and what the audit described as executive mismanagement. The financial losses forced several Saccos to write off millions of shillings invested in KUSCCO. Stima DT SACCO wrote off Sh108 million, while Balozi SACCO wrote off Sh437.5 million and Afa SACCO Sh361.6 million, among others. The Government has since been overseeing efforts to address KUSCCO's financial position and strengthen governance of the organisation. The special general meeting is expected to provide shareholders with an update on the union's status as the Government moves to restructure its operations under the proposed KEFESCO name.
