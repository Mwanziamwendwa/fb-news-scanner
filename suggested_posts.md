**1. Wanyonyi triumphs in Lausanne as stars eye glory in Silesia**

Measured steps and a tactical homestretch push decided the winner of the men's 800m showdown at the Lausanne Diamond League in Switzerland on Friday night. Two-lap greats had blazed around the Pontaise Olympic Stadium track, but it was Olympic and world champion Emmanuel Wanyonyi who stamped his authority. The 800m thriller had kicked off with huge expectations of a memorable show, and that is exactly what the deep field, which included world gold medallist Marco Arop of Canada and rising star Djamel Sedjati of Algeria, served up to expectant fans. However, Sedjati mounted the highest pressure on Wanyonyi in the closing stages of the hot contest in Lausanne. It was expected to be a battle that would have seen the bigwigs reach the 1:41 territory, but that could not be achieved at the Pontaise Olympic Stadium. Wanyonyi won his first Diamond League 800m of the year in 1:42.19 after chasing down Arop and holding off a strong Sedjati. Eventually, Sedjati took second place in a season-best 1:42.31, while Arop settled for third in 1:43.18. Arop set off with a strong showing, racing behind the pacemaker until the bell. The Canadian still could not surrender the lead after the 400m mark, which was reached in 48.86, but he could not sustain it and was overpowered by Wanyonyi and Sedjati in the home straight. Wanyonyi was the favourite, given that he was fresh from setting a world 1000m record in Monaco, and the 800m victory was his second Diamond League meeting win of the season. "I had a battle, especially over the final 100m, but I felt very strong and I´m happy to have won the race. My legs were beginning to tighten, but I kept pushing all the way to the finish. I love competing at this Diamond League meeting in Lausanne because this stadium is one of the best for me. The track was very good and, although the weather was a little cool, the conditions were perfect," Wanyonyi said after victory. Second-placed Sedjati was happy with his performance despite not reaching his 1:41 target.

**3. Five-peat, no defeat: Muthaiga Golf Club cruise to Patron’s Cup title on home soil**

NAIROBI, Kenya, August 23, 2026 – Muthaiga Golf Club held their nerve to claim a fifth straight Patron's Cup crown on the final day of competition on home soil on Sunday evening. The hosts topped the Trophy division with 125.5 points after three days of action, as VetLab Sports Club (83 points) and Railway Club (49.5 points) finished second and third respectively. Having come into the ultimate day with 82 points, Muthaiga still had much to do, with more points up for grabs in the singles competition. Among those who came up good for Muthaiga included Bo Ciera, Jay Sandhu, Kushil Nathwani, Daniel Kiragu who each won two of their singles match-ups to draw the team closer to glory. In the Shield division, Sigona Golf Club earned promotion to the Trophy category after topping the standings with 122 points, 42 more than Thika Sports Club in second, and Nyali Golf and Country Club who came third with 56 points. In the Plaque category, Royal Nairobi pulled off a masterclass of comebacks, recovering from their dour show in the first two days to eventually ascend the leaderboard with 96 points. Golf Park, who had began the day as leaders with 56 points, slipped to second with a close 81.5 points as Kiambu sat third with 80 points. Windsor Golf Hotel and Country Club did just about enough to end the day as Bowl winners, amassing 111.5 points, ahead of Limuru Country Club (78 points) and Karen Country Club (32.5 points) in second and third respectively. The same was the case in the Mug category where overnight leaders Ruiru Sports Club emerged victorious with 112.5 points. Eldoret Club finished second with 79 points as Kericho Golf Club came third with 30.5 points. The competition was held in memory of Kenya's third president Mwai Kibaki, who was an avid golfer and a patron of the Kenya Golf Union.
