**4. KRA Tightens Tax Compliance for Traders With New Ksh 3.2M Import Rule**

The Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) has raised the minimum reference point for general containerised cargo from Ksh2.5 million to Ksh3.2 million to curb under-declaration by some traders during importation. KRA said the new rule took effect on August 20, 2026, after consultations with the Kenya International Freight and Warehousing Association (KIFWA) and representatives of small traders. In a statement on Tuesday, August 25, KRA clarified that raising the minimum reference point to Ksh3.2 million does not mean every container of imported goods is automatically valued at that amount . Instead, the Authority will use the figure as a reference when checking whether traders have correctly declared the value of their goods and paid the right amount of customs tax. "This seeks to establish a more predictable and equitable framework for customs valuation while addressing concerns over the misuse of cargo consolidation arrangements," KRA said. KRA reiterated that the revised benchmark is intended to promote fairness in trade, protect government revenue and ensure compliant businesses are not disadvantaged by traders who evade customs obligations. According to the Authority , cargo consolidation remains a legitimate way for small traders to reduce international shipping costs by combining several shipments in one container. However, the arrangement has previously been abused through practices such as under-valuation, misdescription, misclassification, and concealment of high-value goods. "These practices create revenue leakage and distort competition by allowing non-compliant businesses to bring goods into the country at artificially low costs," the Authority added. KRA warned that such practices allow non-compliant traders to bring goods into the country at artificially low costs, putting businesses that comply with customs requirements at a disadvantage. High-value electronics are among the products that have attracted greater scrutiny, with KRA citing smartphones as an example, noting that high-end phones have been declared as lower-value models to reduce the customs value and taxes payable. KRA said the revised Ksh3.2 million reference point replaces the previous Ksh2.5 million figure, which had remained unchanged despite changes in economic conditions and import values. The Authority said the new figure provides a more current and predictable reference point for customs administration.

**2. Government cautions Kenyans against spoofing fraud**

The Government has warned Kenyans against spoofing scams, where criminals impersonate banks, Safaricom and DCI to steal personal information, banking credentials, OTPs and money. Spoofing is a technique where criminals falsify the phone number or sender ID displayed on a victim's phone to make a call or message appear to come from a trusted person or organisation. In call spoofing, a fraudster can manipulate the caller ID so that a victim sees the number of a bank or other legitimate institution instead of the actual number used to make the call. The fraudster may then claim that the victim's bank or mobile money account has been compromised and ask for personal details, PINs or one-time passwords, or instruct the victim to transfer money to an account or M-Pesa number to supposedly secure their funds. According to the Ministry of Interior and National Administration, some criminals have been using spoofed numbers associated with banks, Safaricom and the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI). They may also make follow-up calls while posing as DCI officers investigating the alleged fraud, leading victims to disclose more information or lose more money. SMS spoofing works in a similar way where criminals manipulate the sender name or number so that a fraudulent message appears to have come from a genuine bank or mobile service provider, sometimes appearing in the same conversation thread as legitimate messages. The messages may contain links directing victims to fake websites where they are prompted to enter banking credentials, allowing criminals to access their accounts. The Government has urged Kenyans not to rely on caller IDs or SMS sender names alone, as they can be spoofed. Members of the public have been advised never to share PINs, passwords, OTPs or card details over the phone or through text messages. Anyone who receives a suspicious call claiming to be from a bank or government agency should hang up and contact the organisation using its official contact details.
