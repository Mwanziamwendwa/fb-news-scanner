**9. Kenya Power Dealt Blow as Court Rules on Power Disconnections in Some Areas**

The High Court has barred Kenya Power from disconnecting electricity to county governments over unpaid bills without first exhausting the government dispute resolution mechanism. In a recent ruling, the court ruled that disputes between Kenya Power and county governments over electricity charges constitute intergovernmental disputes and must be handled in accordance with the Constitution and the Intergovernmental Relations Act. According to the court, Kenya Power is an agency and instrument of the national government in the electricity sector. Therefore, the power company could not avoid the constitutional requirements governing relations between the national and county governments by relying on its corporate status. "The corporate veil cannot be deployed to exempt an organ of national government from a constitutional obligation of comity," the judge ruled. The court ruled that Kenya Power must first report outstanding electricity bills owed by county governments to the Cabinet Secretary for the National Treasury under Section 161 of the Energy Act. The Treasury is then required to report the debts to Parliament for necessary appropriation. "Where Government or its agencies default in paying electricity bills -Without prejudice to any other remedies available, a licensee which has outstanding uncollected billings attributable to the National Government, County Government or any Government agency shall report such billings to the Cabinet Secretary for the National Treasury who shall, in turn, report the same to Parliament for necessary appropriation," the ruling stated. The case arose after Kenya Power disconnected electricity to several Nairobi County offices on February 14, 2025, over unpaid electricity bills. The disconnection sparked a public standoff between the utility and the county before power was restored following mediation convened by the Head of the Public Service. In its ruling, the court also ordered Kenya Power to issue at least 30 days' written notice to the affected county government and public notice before disconnecting essential public installations. The order applies to facilities including hospitals, water and sewerage installations, fire stations, mortuaries and street lighting.

The second E is Explanation, where the candidate explains how the evidence supports the point. L is Link, where the point is linked back to the question. **35. Things parents say about teachers that can destroy a child’s learning journey**

A child's learning journey does not begin and end at the school gate. It is shaped by what happens in the classroom, what happens at home and, most importantly, by the relationship between the two. Children listen to their parents more closely than many adults realise. They absorb conversations, attitudes and emotions. A parent may make a frustrated comment about a teacher and forget about it a few minutes later, but the child may carry those words into school the following morning. That single comment can influence how the child receives instruction, responds to correction and relates to the teacher. A teacher may spend hours building a child's confidence, encouraging discipline and nurturing curiosity, only for that effort to be weakened by negative messages at home. This is why parents need to be careful about how they discuss teachers in the presence of their children. Parents have every right to question teachers. They have a responsibility to investigate genuine complaints and protect their children from mistreatment. They should never ignore bullying, abuse, discrimination or other serious concerns. However, there is a significant difference between protecting a child and poisoning a child's perception of education. A parent can disagree with a teacher without destroying the teacher's credibility. A parent can challenge a decision without encouraging disrespect. A parent can listen to a child's complaint without automatically declaring the teacher guilty. The following statements can quietly damage a child's learning journey. “Your teacher doesn't like you” This can plant a dangerous idea in a child's mind. Once a child believes a teacher dislikes them, ordinary classroom experiences may suddenly appear personal. A correction becomes hatred. A low mark becomes targeting. A request to improve becomes evidence that the teacher is against them. If there is genuine evidence of unfair treatment, investigate it carefully. But do not create suspicion before establishing the facts. Children should be encouraged to distinguish between a teacher correcting their behaviour and a teacher attacking their character. “Don't listen to everything your teacher says” There is nothing wrong with teaching children to think critically.
