**6. 4 arrested over deaths of 18 elephants in Amboseli as finding point to cyanide poisoning**

NAIROBI, Kenya, Aug 23 – Four suspects have been arrested as persons of interest in investigations into the deaths of 18 elephants in the Amboseli ecosystem, with preliminary laboratory findings pointing to probable cyanide poisoning. The first fifteen deaths were recorded between June 24 and July 24, prompting the Kenya Wildlife Service, security, investigative and scientific agencies to intensify investigations. “Thus far, comprehensive field investigations, laboratory analysis and environmental sampling have been undertaken,” Tourism and Wildlife Cabinet Secretary Rebecca Miano said in a stament released Saturday evening. “Preliminary laboratory results point to the probable involvement of a toxic substance, with earlier indications suggesting cyanide poisoning arising from the ingestion of pesticide-contaminated tomatoes sourced from farms adjacent to wildlife habitats.” Miano said the findings “remain subject to further scientific verification before the investigation is deemed complete.” Lobbies seek cyanide poisoning probe after Amboseli elephant deaths The multi-agency investigation has so far led to the arrest of four suspects, with investigators pursuing an additional individual believed to have information relevant to the case. A search in Tikondo, Kimana, within Loitoktok Sub-County, also resulted in the recovery of assorted substances, including chemicals believed to have been sourced from a neighbouring country. The materials have been secured for further scientific analysis. Miano, however, cautioned against treating the arrests as proof of wrongdoing. “Although the investigations remain active, the arrest of persons of interest and the recovery of exhibits do not in themselves establish criminal culpability,” she said. “The Government will allow the investigative and judicial processes to proceed to their lawful conclusion.” She said anyone established to have unlawfully killed, poisoned or harmed wildlife would be prosecuted in accordance with the law. Miano described the deaths as “not solely a matter concerning elephants,” but a national wildlife conservation and security concern. “The government is reinforcing measures to safeguard wildlife across all these ecosystems,” she said, adding that agencies would strengthen intelligence-led protection, surveillance, scientific investigation, law enforcement and rapid response in vulnerable areas.
